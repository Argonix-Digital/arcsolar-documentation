Every customer moves through sales, then delivery, then close-out. At each handover, make sure the next step has an owner and a date.

## The eight stages

The signpost icon in the top bar (**Customer lifecycle**) shows these stages at any time.

| Stage | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Lead** | Assigned and awaiting first contact |
| **Qualified** | Needs confirmed and opportunity qualified |
| **Site assessed** | Site information ready for the quote |
| **Quoted** | Quote sent, outcome pending |
| **Deal won** | Accepted deal awaiting handover |
| **Operations** | Compliance, ordering and installer preparation |
| **Installation** | Scheduled through to installation |
| **Complete** | Handover done and support active |

## From enquiry to quote

1. **Capture the enquiry.** Find or create the [customer record](/customers/records). Confirm contact details, source, consent and owner.
2. **Have the conversation.** Call or text from [Comms](/customers/calls-sms), record the outcome and set the next [task](/customers/tasks).
3. **Understand the site.** Request the [bill, photos and cable-run video](/quoting/site-information). Review what comes back.
4. **Prepare the offer.** [Create a quote](/quoting/walkthrough), check the customer preview and send it.

## From decision to installation

Track the quote's viewed, accepted and paid states separately. Let [automatic follow-ups](/quoting/follow-ups) support the rep's own calls.

Once the customer accepts, open the linked [project](/delivery/projects). Confirm the scope, funding, equipment and installer before you set a date. Use [Calendar](/delivery/calendar) and [Field Operations](/delivery/field-operations) to plan the work.

## Close the loop

Collect installation evidence, finish compliance and [rebates](/delivery/rebates), hand over to the customer and [reconcile payments](/delivery/finance).

<Note>
An accepted quote is not a paid deposit. A paid deposit does not mean the job is ready to install. A finished site visit does not close the project.
</Note>

## Leave a useful handover

Write down what was agreed, what is missing, who acts next and when. Put open work in a task with an owner, so the next person can pick it up.
