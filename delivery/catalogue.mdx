**Open:** [**Product Catalogue**](https://app.arcsolar.com.au/dashboard/product-catalogue) under **Operations** in the sidebar.

## Review a product

1. Open the product and read its name, brand, model, category, specifications, warranty, images and status.
2. Review the default price, effective dates, labour or add-on records, regional pricing and supplier information.
3. Change only a field you can support with the current price list.
4. Save the draft and review any warning, missing-data or duplicate message.

## Add a product

1. Choose **Add product**.
2. Enter the identity, category and specifications.
3. Add the default price and effective dates.
4. Add images and the technical documents staff need.
5. Save and use it in a sample quote. Confirm the quantity, unit and exclusions.

## Prices belong to a selling brand

One product carries **one price per selling brand**. Adding a brand does not copy the catalogue; it starts that brand's price book empty, and a product only reaches a quote when it has a price in the price book of the brand the quote is sold under.

### Read the pricing status labels

Every product row carries a **Status** badge that describes the price **in the brand you are looking at**, not merely whether a number exists somewhere:

| Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | This brand prices the product today, so quotes under it can use the price |
| **Draft price** | A price exists but has not been published, so quotes ignore it |
| **Future price** | A published price starts on a later date; nothing applies today |
| **Expired price** | A published price has already ended |
| **Upon request** | Deliberately not priced — quoted by arrangement |
| **Priced in BRAND** | Priced, but in **another** selling brand's price book — so it will not appear in this brand's quotes until you price it here |
| **Missing price** | No sellable price book prices or quotes it at all |
| **Missing price book** | There is no price book to resolve against |

A **Priced in BRAND** badge is not an error. It is the normal state of a correctly priced product in a brand that does not carry it, and it is what keeps the two brands' prices independent. The full vocabulary, including the lifecycle badges **Draft product**, **Inactive**, **Review due** and **System pricing**, is listed in [Prices per brand](/setup/price-books).

A brand's own **Products and prices** list words the same states in book terms — **Published**, **Draft only**, **Missing price**, **No price book** — because that surface is about one book.

### Set a price for the selected brand

1. Open the product while the correct brand is selected, or filter the catalogue to that brand.
2. Enter the price for the brand you are looking at, with its effective date.
3. Choose **Set price now** or **Schedule for a date**, then save.
4. Check the row now shows **Current** (or **Draft price** if you saved a draft).
5. Make a sample quote under that brand and check the price.

If you are preparing a batch of a new brand's prices, the **Selling brands** page in Settings can copy prices from the original brand into the new book **as drafts** — see [Prices per brand](/setup/price-books). Nothing is published by the copy itself, so every copied price is still reviewed before it reaches a quote.

### Which brand a product row is priced in

Use the catalogue's brand filter to switch between price books. The filter changes the prices the table shows; it does not change the records, so a product that looks unpriced in one brand remains priced in the other.

## Change or retire a product

Confirm the replacement price, effective date and impacted packages. Preview a quote with the new price and check whether active quotes need revision. Do not delete a product with active usage; mark it inactive instead.

## Bulk and imports

Select **Import Products** (under **Tools**) to add or update many products from an XLSX or CSV file. Imported prices are drafts unless `publish_price` is `TRUE`. See [Set up your pricing](/setup/pricing#import-from-a-spreadsheet) for the steps and rules.
